Starting pitching

Kevin Gausman’s rate profile gives him a clear advantage over Andrew Abbott. In his last 118 batters faced, Gausman has struck out 22.0% while walking only 5.1%, producing a 3.69 rate-based expected ERA against a 4.38 actual mark. Abbott’s recent window tells a very different story: a 16.0% strikeout rate, an 11.2% walk rate, and an 8.8% barrel rate that all point to an expected ERA near 4.95, far above his 4.15 surface number. Both starters typically work into the sixth inning, so the matchup should hinge on Gausman’s superior command and Abbott’s vulnerability to free passes and hard contact.

The lineups

The Cubs lineup carries a collective .856 OPS against left-handed pitching, a 79-point split edge driven by hitters like Seiya Suzuki (.966 OPS vs LHP), Carson Kelly (.914), and Pete Crow-Armstrong (.946). Nearly every spot in the order has produced against southpaws, with only Nico Hoerner (.712) and Michael Busch (.760) sitting below .775. The Reds’ .716 OPS versus right-handers is dragged down by TJ Friedl (.503), Jose Trevino (.666), and Matt McLain (.610), though Elly De La Cruz (.755) and Sal Stewart (.781) offer some thump at the top. The platoon mismatch heavily favors Chicago.

Bullpens

Both relief corps enter in rough shape. The Cubs bullpen has logged only 6.0 innings over the last three days but carries a 10.5 ERA and 1.83 WHIP in that stretch, while the Reds pen has been worked harder (9.0 IP, 9 relievers used) and posted a 10.0 ERA and 1.89 WHIP. Neither team lists arms as unavailable, but the heavy recent usage and inflated run prevention mean any lead is fragile once the starters exit. This factor primarily raises the expected total rather than providing a decisive edge to either side.

Form and regression

The Cubs lineup’s 30-day xwOBA on contact sits at .341, a touch below their season norms and flagged as ‘cold,’ but still respectable. The Reds hitters have actually fared better by expected contact quality, with a .369 xwOBA, a 35.4% hard-hit rate, and a 9.4% barrel rate, though they strike out 25.3% of the time. For the starters, Gausman’s underlying numbers (3.69 expected ERA) suggest his 4.38 ERA overstates his struggles, while Abbott’s 4.15 ERA grossly understates his true performance level; his rate-based expectation of 4.95 implies significant negative regression is coming. The form data thus points to a pitcher-friendly correction for the Cubs and a looming fade for Abbott.

The case against this pick

For the pick to be wrong, Abbott would need to once again defy his peripherals by limiting walks and suppressing hard contact, keeping his ERA well below the 4.95 his rates imply. The Reds’ lineup has a higher collective xwOBA (.369 vs. .341) and barrel rate (9.4% vs. 6.6%) than the Cubs, so if Gausman’s own 39.5% hard-hit rate gets punished early, Cincinnati could build a lead before the bullpens enter. The Cubs’ lineup, despite its platoon edge, is flagged as cold, and a quiet day from the top of the order would leave the game in the hands of two heavily worked, high-ERA bullpens where randomness can swing the result. The input I trust least is Abbott’s extreme ERA–underlying-rate gap; if his luck survives another start, the pitching equation flips.
